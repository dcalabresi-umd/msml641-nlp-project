\begin{abstract}
This project analyzes NLP techniques for predicting headline performance using the Upworthy Research Archive dataset. We compare traditional NLP methods (ranging from bi-grams to BERT) with LLM-based approaches for click-through rate (CTR) prediction. Our analysis includes feature engineering, topic modeling using BERTopic, and evaluation of various linguistic features on headline success. We examine the relationship between sentiment, topic, readability, and other text characteristics with CTR, while controlling for confounders through A/B test analysis.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Topic Modeling}

We chose BERTopic \cite{grootendorst2022bertopic} for topic modeling as it represents the state-of-the-art approach for short texts. BERTopic's pipeline includes: (1) BERT embeddings for text representation, (2) UMAP for dimensionality reduction, (3) HDBSCAN for clustering, (4) tokenization of clustered headlines, and (5) c-TF-IDF weighting for topic representation.

Traditional methods like LDA are unsuited for short texts like headlines, and while GSDMM is designed for short texts, BERTopic has shown superior performance and interpretability.

\subsection{Literature Validation}

Our results partially contradict published findings on sentiment. While \citet{robertson2023negativity} and \citet{munger2023linguistic} found significant negative sentiment effects using LIWC, our VADER-based analysis found no statistically significant correlation. This discrepancy may stem from: (1) different sentiment analysis tools (LIWC vs. VADER), (2) different methodological approaches, or (3) VADER being more appropriate for short texts.
